§ Project 02 — 2025

Predicting water main failure 6–12 months out.

Client
Western Virginia Water Authority
Role
Data Scientist (independent IC)
Year
Jan – Jul 2025
Stack
Python · scikit-learn · XGBoost · ArcGIS · PowerBI
Data
20+ yrs CMMS · environmental · operational
Outcome
~$150K directed value
Audience
Engineering · GIS · Executive
Status
Deployed · 5-yr capital plan input

The problem

Western Virginia's water system is aging. Replacing every pipe is fiscally impossible; reacting to failures is operationally painful and expensive. The Authority needed a way to point its capital plan at the assets most likely to fail next — without inventing a budget it didn't have.

Approach

I integrated three streams the team had never joined before: twenty-plus years of CMMS maintenance history, environmental factors (soil, freeze cycles, traffic load), and operational telemetry. I trained a gradient-boosted model to score every active main on its probability of failure within a 6–12 month window, then surfaced the top-risk assets through a dashboard the engineering team could act on.

  • Joined CMMS, GIS, and environmental data into a single feature store
  • Trained an XGBoost classifier with time-series cross-validation
  • Calibrated probabilities so risk scores were directly comparable across asset classes
  • Shipped a PowerBI surface tied to live CMMS so the model output never went stale

Outcome

The first ranked list directed proactive replacement of high-risk mains and added approximately $150K in value through avoided emergency repairs and better-sequenced capital work. The dashboard now feeds the Authority's 5-year capital plan and gives engineering, GIS, HR, and administration a shared view of asset health.

What I'd do next

Two things. First, fold in real-time pressure-sensor data so the model sees acute precursors, not just slow degradation. Second, expose the model's reasoning to operators with feature attributions per asset, so the recommendation is auditable in the field — trust matters more than F1 once a system is live.

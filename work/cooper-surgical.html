§ Project 01 — 2025 – present

A data spine the C-suite can interrogate live.

Client
CooperSurgical (Medical Device)
Role
Business Data Scientist, Commercial Excellence
Year
Jul 2025 – present
Stack
Python · SQL · Azure · PowerBI · ARIMA / SARIMAX / Prophet / XGBoost
Data
Transaction records · 20+ product lines
Outcome
Quota across 70+ managers · real-time exec reporting
Audience
C-suite (Marketing · Sales · Business Analytics)
Status
In-flight · running AOP cycle

The problem

Annual planning at a global medical-device business is a needle-threading exercise between top-line growth targets, sales-force capacity, and product-mix reality. The team wanted a data spine that could survive C-suite scrutiny — not a spreadsheet passed around by email until someone lost the version.

Approach

I own the analytics behind the Annual Operating Plan end-to-end: quota allocation across 70+ managers, ensemble forecasting across 20+ product lines, and the pipelines and executive surfaces that hold all of it together. No single model owns the truth — the forecast is a committee, and the committee shows its work.

  • Rebuilt the AOP process end-to-end, from transaction records to executive readout
  • Stood up a PowerBI / SQL / Azure surface that replaced ad-hoc pulls with a live view
  • Standardized ensemble forecasting (ARIMA, SARIMAX, Prophet, XGBoost) across 20+ product lines
  • Aligned quota, territory, and capacity into one coherent view for 70+ managers
  • Ran scenario and sensitivity analysis to stress-test decisions before they hit a slide

Outcome

The AOP now runs on a data spine that leadership can interrogate live. Product-launch decisions get vetted against the forecasting stack before they hit a slide, and executive reporting time collapsed from days of ad-hoc pulls into real-time performance monitoring. The C-suite trusts the numbers because the numbers show their work — every forecast comes with its uncertainty, and every scenario tags the assumptions it hangs on.

What I'd do next

A closed-loop between the forecast and the sales system so drift is caught within a cycle instead of at year-end reconciliation. And more automated attribution — separating pricing, mix, and volume effects at the product-line level, so when a number moves, we know why.

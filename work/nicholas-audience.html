§ Project 04

More engagement, less spend, and a curriculum that heard back.

Duke Nicholas School of the Environment · Data & Marketing Specialist · Oct 2023 – Oct 2024

audience engagement

−20%

6mo

Python · Tableau · ArcGIS

Objective

The Nicholas School was promoting executive environmental-education programs to a wide funnel with a fixed marketing budget. Engagement was mediocre, spend was inefficient, and the team wasn't sure which audience segments were even the right ones to be spending against. The instinct was there; the evidence wasn't.

Approach

I ran the segmentation work as if it were a research project rather than a marketing exercise — multivariate analysis to isolate high-value cohorts, ArcGIS overlays to surface where interest clustered vs. where it was latent, and an automated Python reporting pipeline so optimizations weren't blocked on hand-pulled numbers each week.

  • Ran multivariate segmentation on registrations, engagement, and campaign response
  • Mapped patterns geospatially in ArcGIS — where the audience already was, and where it wasn't yet
  • Automated the campaign-reporting pipeline in Python so iteration was daily, not monthly
  • Fed insights back to curriculum leads, not just marketing — the same signal answered two different questions

Outcome

Engagement rose roughly 40% and spend dropped roughly 20% within six months. The same insights fed curriculum decisions — which programs to prioritize, which to rework — extending the value of the work well past its original marketing brief. Analytics that only serve the department that commissioned it is analytics that hasn't finished.

What I'd do next

Multi-touch attribution. Which channels are actually converting the high-value segment, and which are just showing up in the last-click? That's a harder question than segmentation and needs more instrumented data than a marketing team usually has — but it's the natural next scope.

§ Project 07

The only metric that counted was animals in homes.

The Blue Cross of India · Lead data scientist, pro bono · 2022

adoption lift
~5%
engagement length
6mo

Python · Tableau · Excel

Objective

The Blue Cross of India runs one of the country's largest animal-welfare operations. Records for rescues, adoptions, and campaigns lived in fragmented spreadsheets, which meant good instincts and slow feedback loops. When an adoption drive worked, no one could quickly say why; when one didn't, no one could quickly say what to change.

Approach

Consolidate first, then predict, then act. I built a single data pipeline for rescue and adoption records, ran predictive analytics on which animals had the highest adoption potential given care and marketing lift, and helped design targeted outreach informed by the model rather than by roster order. The point was to leave the team with a running system, not a one-off report.

  • Built a pipeline processing rescue and adoption records across streams
  • Ran predictive analytics to prioritize campaign effort where it would move the needle
  • Helped design targeted outreach informed by the model, not by intake date
  • Trained the team on the workflow so it would keep running after the engagement ended

Outcome

Adoption rates lifted approximately 5% over the six months of the engagement. In shelter terms that's animals in homes instead of kennels — the only metric that actually counted. The system kept running after I left, which is the second metric that actually counts.

What I'd do next

Longitudinal follow-up. Do the adoptions stick? A pipeline that also tracks post-adoption outcomes — returns, welfare check-ins, foster-to-adopt conversions — would tell you whether the marketing lift is real or is quietly recycling the same animals through the system.

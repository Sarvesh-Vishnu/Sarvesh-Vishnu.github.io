Finding the water mains most likely to fail next.

Western Virginia Water Authority · Data Scientist · January to July 2025

The idea

You can’t replace every pipe.

So the order matters. Set a budget, then list the mains two ways: by the model, or oldest first. Point at any main to read what the model sees there.

Listed by the model, about half of the picks are from the oldest era. The rest are younger mains where clay soil, past breaks or a busy road raise the risk.

The real model was gradient-boosted on 20+ years of maintenance history, with soil, freeze cycles and traffic load beside it. This drawing uses a simple stand-in to show one thing: age is a signal, and it is not the only one.

Objective

Water mains across Western Virginia, and much of the United States, are ageing. Replacing every pipe is not affordable, and waiting for failures is painful and expensive. The Authority needed a way to point its capital plan at the mains most likely to fail next, within the budget it already had.

Approach

I joined three streams that had not been joined before: twenty-plus years of maintenance history from the CMMS (the computerised maintenance management system), environmental factors such as soil, freeze cycles and traffic load, and operational telemetry. A gradient-boosted model scored every active main on its likelihood of failing within six to twelve months. The highest-risk mains went to a dashboard the asset-management and engineering teams could act on.

  • Integrated CMMS, GIS and environmental data into a single feature store
  • Trained an XGBoost classifier with time-series cross-validation, so it was always tested on years it had not seen
  • Calibrated the probabilities so risk scores could be compared across asset classes
  • Shipped a Power BI view tied to the live CMMS, so the list never went stale

Outcome

The first ranked list directed proactive replacement of high-risk mains and added approximately $150K in value through avoided emergency repairs and better-sequenced capital work. The dashboard now feeds the Authority’s five-year capital plan and gives engineering, GIS, HR and administration a shared view of asset health.

What I’d do next

Two things. First, fold in real-time pressure-sensor data so the model sees sudden warning signs as well as slow wear. Second, show operators the model’s reasons for each main, so a recommendation can be checked in the field. Once a system is live, trust matters more than a test score.

Personal project · Numit is “sun” in Meitei

A globe you can sit with.

Personal build · Design, engineering, editorial · 2025 – 2026 · Live site · Essay

The idea

Same world, different facing.

Each mark is one event from Numit’s curated list. A red mark is shadow: something breaking. A plain mark is light: something being repaired, often in the same place. Turn the globe, and point at a mark to read it.

This face of the world holds 293 of Numit’s 356 shadow events and 70 of its 91 light events. The most are in India, Myanmar and Mali.

Every mark is one event in Numit’s curated list, with a named source behind it: 356 of breaking and 91 of repair, as the world stood on 23 October 2026. 37 of the light events say which break they grew out of.

Objective

The news doesn't invite you to sit. Caring at internet scale isn't really possible, and the fleeting helplessness of watching the world burn compresses into what psychologists call psychic numbing — the pattern where suffering, past a certain volume, stops registering as human at all. A statistic is what's left of a person after you've stopped paying attention.

Approach

Numit is a contemplative globe that maps two faces of the world. The shadow: places that are breaking right now. The light: some of the same places being put back together. You encounter the crisis first, and the response second — because hope handed to us before we've sat with the weight of something is decorative. Hope we arrive at afterwards is different. It lands as relief.

  • Hand-built WebGL globe in Three.js, not a wrapper library — every arc, every marker, every camera transition is deliberate
  • A curated list of several hundred events, each one placed, dated and checked by hand against a named source: UN agencies, Human Rights Watch, Reuters, Al Jazeera and more than a hundred other outlets
  • A live news feed that I built and then switched off. It placed each story in the country of the outlet that published it, which moved the world’s crises to Washington and London. The curated list is the globe until that is solved
  • A source-credibility index of my own, informed by NewsGuard’s public method and not its licensed scores. An outlet that scores under 75 never reaches the globe
  • A representation floor checked on every build: eighteen countries and regions, the United States, China, Russia and the Gulf states among them, must each keep at least one shadow event. Left to news volume, rich and powerful states would look clean
  • The globe wears its own date. “World as of” is read from the newest event in the list, so the site cannot claim to be fresher than its data
  • Transparent compositing: any person described is a disclosed composite from real reporting, never fictional
  • Engagement metrics aggregated through Upstash Redis — the site never watches individuals

Outcome

There isn't a conversion metric on Numit, and there was never going to be one. The success criterion is qualitative: if one person leaves having chosen a place, learned a name, and done one small real thing, the whole experience has paid for itself. The full essay lives on Substack.

What I'd do next

Regional editorial rotations so the shadow layer surfaces underreported crises, not just the ones with news volume. And an offline reading mode for places with unstable connectivity — the site should still work where the news does.

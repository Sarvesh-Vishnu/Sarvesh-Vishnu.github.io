The method

One story, from the week’s sources to Monday’s inbox.

Each week the companion reads public sources and writes a short brief for leadership. Step through what happens to one story: gathered, joined, weighed, drafted, checked and sent.

Send. The item reaches leadership’s inboxes on Monday morning. It also stays searchable, so a leader can ask about the topic later and get the story back with its sources.

One story is shown. Every item in the Monday brief goes through the same steps, and nothing internal goes into any of them.

The sources, the story and the draft are written for this page. No company news, internal data or output of the real system appears here.

Objective

Leaders need to know what changed outside the company each week: new laws and reimbursement rules, regulator decisions, trial results, published research, and what competitors announced. Reading all of it takes hours, and a summary nobody can check is worse than none.

Approach

I built the companion as a graph in LangGraph, one node for each step of the team’s weekly review, so every run can be retraced step by step.

  • Gathers public sources only: legislation, regulators, trial registries, published research and trade press. Nothing internal is indexed.
  • Splits each document into short passages and retrieves with hybrid search: keywords (BM25) for exact names and bill numbers, embeddings for paraphrase, fused by reciprocal rank.
  • Joins items that tell the same story, and recognises a story covered in an earlier issue even under a new headline.
  • Scores each story for materiality and confidence. Confidence comes from the kind of source: a bill or a regulator outranks a press release repeated by trade press.
  • Drafts each item from its own passages only. The output schema allows a citation only to a passage the model was given.
  • Checks every claim, number and date in code, then has a second model critique the draft. A draft that still fails is replaced by an extract of its sources.
  • Routes low-confidence or sensitive items to a person before anything is sent.
  • Treats source text as data, never as instructions, which closes the obvious prompt-injection route into a tool that advises executives.

Two ways in

The brief reaches leaders’ inboxes every Monday morning. Between issues, a leader can ask about a specific topic and get back the stories on it, each with its sources, from the same index.

Outcome

Since it started in 2026, five members of the executive team, from senior directors to the Head of Sales, have read one sourced brief each Monday instead of chasing the week’s news. Between issues they can pull the background on a topic without commissioning a research request.

What I’d do next

Measure it week by week. A labelled set of past issues would score retrieval and citation accuracy on every run, and a one-tap rating on each item would show which stories leaders actually used, so the ranking learns from them.

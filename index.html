§ 03 — About

Strategy, modeled.

I work at the intersection of data, analytics, and strategy — and the more interesting bits are usually where that intersection runs into business, social sciences, and the messier parts of the world. My day job is at Cooper Surgical, where I own analytics behind the medical-device Annual Operating Plan: quota allocation across 75+ territory managers, ensemble forecasting across 25+ product lines, and the pipelines that hold all of it together.

Before that I built failure-prediction ML for Western Virginia's water infrastructure (~$150K in directed value), ran NLP sentiment analysis at Pinnacle Fertility for my Duke capstone, led a Deloitte analyst pod through a Hadoop and PowerBI engagement, and shipped a control-system NLP proof-of-concept at Siemens.

I studied Quantitative Management at Duke's Fuqua School (Strategy track, Merit Scholar) after a CS & AI–ML degree at SRM. The throughline: technical depth that survives a C-suite conversation, and a preference for pragmatic decisions over clever models.

Outside the dashboards, I volunteer in animal welfare (the Blue Cross of India engagement started there) and led a 150-person socio-economic non-profit through college. I read more social science than is strictly necessary for the job — causal inference, behavioural econ, the quieter end of strategy — and keep a foot in the arts. The best models I've built have always come out of taking a non-technical question seriously first.
